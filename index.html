Country Instability Board

CII v8 methodology adopted from the open-source World Monitor project — their formula and baselines, our snapshot's event codings
Adopted formula (CII v8) · event = Unrest·0.25 + Conflict·0.30 + Security·0.20 + Information·0.25, then CII = baseline·0.40 + event·0.60 + boosts, floored (UCDP active war → min 70) and clamped 0–100. Baseline values are World Monitor's published per-country baselineRisk table; component codings (0–100) come from this snapshot's readings; boosts follow their published caps (sanctions ≤14, displacement ≤20, alert blend ≤25). Levels use their bands: Critical 81–100 · High 66–80 · Elevated 51–65 · Normal 31–50 · Low 0–30.

Validation notes: the CII board and our independent Conflict Monitor agree on every overlapping call (war-state countries all floor at 70+; US–China reads as commercial, not kinetic). One coverage divergence found and kept: World Monitor's Tier-1 set omits Sudan — arguably the world's worst active war — so Sudan appears on our Conflict Monitor but cannot be CII-scored without inventing a baseline. Their bias-prevention design (separating news pressure from conflict scoring, floors against data gaps) independently mirrors this page's fixed-rubric rule.

CNP Board · 综合国力

Comprehensive National Power — the CCP's own quantified doctrine for gauging state power, adopted as a scoring framework
Adopted methodology · CNP (zonghe guoli) was coined in 1985 by Huang Shuofeng of the PLA Academy of Military Science — "the total strength, material and non-material, that a sovereign state wields to ensure its survival and development" — and operationalized as weighted indices by AMS, CASS (Wang Songfen's 8 categories × 64 indicators) and CICIR. This board uses Huang's seven components with a disclosed weight reconstruction: economy 0.28 is Huang's published weight and diplomacy 0.07 is CASS's published weight; the five remaining weights are interpolated to follow the CASS ordering (defense/S&T above resources/politics/culture) and sum to 1. CNP = Σ wᵢ·cᵢ, components coded 0–100 from structural facts plus this snapshot's readings. Huang's own caveat is honored in spirit: he insisted the system is non-linear and "small changes in initial balance cause huge changes in results" — so treat the single number as a doctrine's lens, not physics.

Model integration (methodology v1.1): the computed China/US CNP ratio now feeds Graham Allison's "structural stress" indicator on the Theory Scoreboard — the trap's defining variable (rising power's strength approaching the ruling power's) measured with the rising power's own yardstick. This is the first addition to the tracked-metrics pool since the model was fixed; indicator sets, weights, rubric and thresholds of the theory model itself are unchanged.

Theory Scoreboard

Computed, not judged — scores, verdicts and ranks are calculated by this page from the indicator dataset below
Scoring model · S = 100 · Σ(wᵢ·oᵢ) / Σwᵢ — each theory is decomposed into indicators; outcome oᵢ is coded on a fixed rubric (1 hit · 0.75 mostly · 0.5 partial/open · 0.25 weak · 0 miss) against the cited reading; weight wᵢ = 2 for the theory's core claim, 1 for auxiliary predictions. Verdict labels are threshold-mapped from the score: ≥75 Supported · 55–74 Watch · 40–54 Mixed · <40 Weak. No discretionary points — every number on a card below is computed at render time from the table it sits above. Each card carries two computed scores: this cycle (the snapshot indicators, weighted — this drives the ranking) and since 1945 (major post-WW2 events coded on the same rubric, equal weights — the theory's historical track record, expandable below each card).

Forecast Board

Probabilistic predictions computed from base rates + documented adjustments — every number auditable, every forecast falsifiable
Forecasting method · Each probability is computed in-page as p = clamp(base + Σ adjustments, 0.02, 0.98). The base rate is an outside-view reference class (how often events of this type resolve this way historically); each adjustment (±0.03–0.15, itemized on the card) is tied to a specific reading from the data sections or an event from the historical record; conditional forecasts state their condition. This is the toolkit with actual forecasting-tournament pedigree (Good Judgment Project): reference classes, structured adjustments, and calibration scoring. No machine-learning model runs here by design — with one world and ~20 inputs, a fitted model would be pseudo-precision wearing a lab coat. Accountability is real instead: every forecast carries an ID and a deadline, and resolved forecasts get Brier-scored (p − outcome)² on future refreshes — the board's own calibration becomes data. Where a liquid prediction market exists (Polymarket, Kalshi, CME FedWatch), its price appears on the card as an independent market check, and a blended estimate is computed with the Good Judgment aggregation extremize(mean, a=1.73) — see Adopted Frameworks below.
Calibration ledger · 0 resolved · Brier score: — (populates as deadlines pass on future refreshes; lower is better, 0.25 = coin-flipping, ≤0.15 = decent forecaster)

Method

The scoreboard is a deterministic scoring model: indicators, weights, the outcome rubric, and the verdict thresholds are fixed in the page's dataset, and the page's own JavaScript computes every score, verdict and rank from them — identical inputs always produce identical outputs, and you can audit the arithmetic in the page source. Indicator readings are drawn from this snapshot's tracked data: equity indices, WCI/BDI and spot routes, IATA demand, AI capex and memory allocation, chip-export controls, sanctions actions, tariff schedules, trade deals, EV capacity, and the UCDP conflict count.

Where judgment remains, it is confined and disclosed: (a) model design — which indicators operationalize each theory, the rubric, and the core-claim weighting, all fixed before scoring; and (b) outcome coding — mapping each cited reading onto the five-level rubric. Dispute any row and the score recomputes mechanically from your corrected coding.

Published pages cannot fetch external data (platform restriction), so this is a timestamped snapshot with the age counter above tracking staleness. To refresh: ask Claude to "update the dashboard" — new readings are pulled, outcomes re-coded against the same rubric, and this URL republished in place.
